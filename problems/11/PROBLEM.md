  <claim>
    For every integer \(d\ge 2\), let \([F_{\mathrm Z},0]\) denote the
    zero-displacement Zauner Clifford class whose symplectic part induces
    \[
      F_{\mathrm Z}(p,q)=(q-p,-p)
    \]
    on the \(d\)-dimensional Weyl-Heisenberg labels. There exists a unit vector
    \(|\phi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^d\) that is an eigenvector of a unitary
    representative \(U_{[F_{\mathrm Z},0]}\) of this fixed Clifford class, and
    the Weyl-Heisenberg orbit projectors
    \[
      \Pi_{p,q}
      :=
      D_{p,q}|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|D_{p,q}^{*},
      \qquad p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_d,
    \]
    satisfy
    \[
      \operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{p,q}\Pi_{p',q'})
      =
      \frac{d\,\delta_{p,p'}\delta_{q,q'}+1}{d+1}
      \qquad
      \text{for all }p,q,p',q'\in\mathbb{Z}_d .
    \]
  </claim>

  <def>
    Let \(\mathbb{Z}_d=\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\), let
    \(\{|q\rangle:q\in\mathbb{Z}_d\}\) be the computational basis of
    \(\mathbb{C}^d\), and set \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/d)\). Define the shift and
    clock unitaries by
    \[
      X|q\rangle=|q+1\rangle,\qquad Z|q\rangle=\omega^q|q\rangle,
      \qquad q\in\mathbb{Z}_d,
    \]
    with arithmetic modulo \(d\). For \(p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_d\), write
    \(D_{p,q}=Z^pX^q\). Multiplying \(D_{p,q}\) by a scalar phase does not
    change the projector \(D_{p,q}|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|D_{p,q}^{*}\). The
    Weyl-Heisenberg group \(\mathcal{H}_d\) is the subgroup of \(U(d)\)
    generated by \(X\), \(Z\), and scalar phases.

    A Weyl-Heisenberg fiducial vector is a unit vector \(|\phi\rangle\) whose
    \(d^2\) orbit projectors \(\Pi_{p,q}\) satisfy the trace equations in the
    claim. The corresponding SIC-POVM consists of the effects
    \(E_{p,q}=d^{-1}\Pi_{p,q}\). These equations make the projectors
    \(d^2\) equiangular lines in \(\mathbb{C}^d\) and imply that the effects
    form a rank-one symmetric informationally complete positive
    operator-valued measure.

    The Clifford normalizer is
    \[
      \mathcal{C}_d
      :=
      \{U\in U(d):U\mathcal{H}_dU^{*}=\mathcal{H}_d\}.
    \]
    Its elements act on the labels \((p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_d^2\) modulo scalar
    phases. In Appleby's Clifford-label notation, a Clifford class has both a
    symplectic label action and a displacement parameter, defined using the
    normalized Weyl displacement convention. In this entry, the Zauner Clifford
    class is the fixed zero-displacement class \([F_{\mathrm Z},0]\): the
    displacement parameter is zero before quotienting by scalar phases, not
    merely unspecified. Its symplectic part is the OQP Problem 23 action
    \[
      F_{\mathrm Z}(p,q)=(q-p,-p).
    \]
    A Zauner unitary \(U_{[F_{\mathrm Z},0]}\) is any scalar-phase
    representative in \(\mathcal{C}_d\) of that single projective Clifford
    class. The following relation records the induced label action in the
    present unnormalized Weyl convention; it is not a replacement for the
    zero-displacement condition above:
    \[
      U_{[F_{\mathrm Z},0]}D_{p,q}U_{[F_{\mathrm Z},0]}^{*}
      \in
      \mathbb{T}\,D_{q-p,-p}
      \qquad
      \text{for all }p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_d,
    \]
    where \(\mathbb{T}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|=1\}\). The phases absorb
    Weyl-operator convention choices; they do not allow a nonzero displacement
    parameter or a different canonical order-three Clifford class. This is the
    same projective Clifford operation as the OQP equation
    \(z\,w(p,q)z^*=w(q-p,-p)\). A Zauner subspace is an eigenspace of
    \(U_{[F_{\mathrm Z},0]}\); the eigenvalue may depend on the scalar phase
    chosen for the representative.
  </def>

  <known>
    This entry was collected from Open Quantum Problems, Problem 23, "SIC POVMs
    and Zauner's Conjecture", which the Open Quantum Problems list places in
    the Quantum communication category. OQP Problem 23 presents three variants,
    each stronger than the previous one: arbitrary SIC-POVMs in every finite
    dimension, Weyl-Heisenberg group-covariant SIC-POVMs in every finite
    dimension, and the Zauner formulation asking for a Weyl-Heisenberg
    fiducial vector inside a Zauner eigenspace. The claim in this entry records
    only the strongest of these three OQP variants.

    This entry is distinct from 2, "SIC-POVMs", which records the weaker
    infinite-sequence claim from Horodecki, Rudnicki, and Zyczkowski's Problem 1
    and does not require all dimensions, Weyl-Heisenberg covariance, or a
    Clifford eigenvector fiducial.

    Zauner, "Quantum designs: foundations of a noncommutative design theory",
    International Journal of Quantum Information 9(1), 445-507 (2011), the
    English translation of the 1999 doctoral thesis, conjectured SIC solutions
    in every dimension generated by the Weyl-Heisenberg group with an
    additional order-three symmetry. Renes, Blume-Kohout, Scott, and Caves,
    "Symmetric Informationally Complete Quantum Measurements", Journal of
    Mathematical Physics 45, 2171 (2004), construct SIC-POVMs in dimensions
    \(2,3,4\) and conjecture a group-covariant SIC-POVM in arbitrary dimension,
    supported there by numerical fiducials through dimension \(45\).

    Appleby, "SIC-POVMs and the Extended Clifford Group", Journal of
    Mathematical Physics 46, 052107 (2005), describes the Clifford and
    extended Clifford normalizers and reports that the fiducials known at that
    time, including the numerical fiducials of Renes, Blume-Kohout, Scott, and
    Caves, are eigenvectors of special order-three Clifford unitaries. Scott,
    "SICs: Extending the list of solutions", arXiv:1703.03993 (2017), gives a
    putatively complete numerical list of Weyl-Heisenberg covariant SICs with
    the known symmetries for \(d\le 90\), a solution with Zauner symmetry for
    every \(d\le 121\), and further higher-dimensional solutions. Appleby,
    Chien, Flammia, and Waldron, "Constructing exact symmetric informationally
    complete measurements from numerical solutions", Journal of Physics A 51,
    165302 (2018), construct many additional exact SIC solutions from
    high-precision numerical data. These are finite-dimensional confirmations,
    not an all-dimensions proof.

    Yakymenko, "SICs and the Triangle Group \((3,3,3)\)", SIGMA 20, 044
    (2024), states that the existence of SICs in every dimension remains open
    and studies the canonical order-three unitaries associated with known
    Weyl-Heisenberg SIC examples. Appleby, Flammia, and Kopp, "A Constructive
    Approach to Zauner's Conjecture via the Stark Conjectures",
    arXiv:2501.03970v2 (2025), give a conditional construction of
    Weyl-Heisenberg SICs in all dimensions \(d\gt 3\), assuming an order-one
    abelian Stark conjecture for real quadratic fields and a special-value
    identity for the Shintani-Faddeev modular cocycle.

    Joka, "Symmetric Informationally Complete Positive Operator Valued Measure
    and Zauner conjecture", arXiv:2601.13475v4 (submitted 2026-01-20, revised
    2026-03-07), is a recent preprint whose abstract claims arbitrary SIC-POVMs
    in every finite dimension. This entry does not mark the problem solved
    from that preprint abstract, and the arbitrary SIC existence statement is
    weaker than the Weyl-Heisenberg Clifford-eigenvector claim recorded here.
  </known>

  <ask>
    Prove or disprove the all-dimensions Zauner claim stated above. A proof
    must establish, for every integer \(d\ge 2\), a Weyl-Heisenberg fiducial
    vector lying in an eigenspace of the fixed zero-displacement Zauner Clifford
    class \([F_{\mathrm Z},0]\), and must verify the SIC trace equations for the
    whole orbit. Proving the analogous statement for a nonzero-displacement lift
    or a different canonical order-three Clifford action is progress unless it
    also yields the fixed Zauner class required here.

    To disprove the claim, it is enough to prove that for some finite
    dimension \(d\ge 2\), no eigenvector of the fixed zero-displacement Zauner
    Clifford class \([F_{\mathrm Z},0]\) can be a Weyl-Heisenberg fiducial
    vector. A result showing that no SIC exists at all in some dimension would
    also disprove the claim. A construction of arbitrary non-covariant SICs, or
    of Weyl-Heisenberg fiducials not proved to lie in the fixed Zauner
    eigenspaces, is progress on this entry rather than a proof of the canonical
    claim.
  </ask>
