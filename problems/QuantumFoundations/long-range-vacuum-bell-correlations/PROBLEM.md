  <claim>
    Fix \(m\gt 0\). For the vacuum state \(\omega_{0,m}\) of the massive
    scalar free relativistic Bose field of mass \(m\) on Minkowski spacetime,
    decide whether
    \[
      \Delta_m(L)
      :=
      \sup\{\beta_{\omega_{0,m}}(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B)-2:
      \operatorname{dist}_s(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B)\ge L\}
    \]
    is strictly positive for every finite \(L\gt 0\), where the supremum ranges
    over nonempty bounded localization regions \(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B\)
    with spacelike separated closures and \(\beta_{\omega_{0,m}}\) is the
    optimized CHSH value for the corresponding local algebras.
  </claim>

  <def>
    Work in \(d=1+3\) Minkowski spacetime unless a solution explicitly states a
    different spacetime dimension and proves that the change is immaterial. The
    OQP page does not specify a spacetime dimension; this entry fixes
    \(1+3\), matching the \(\mathbb{R}^4\) convention in the cited
    algebraic-QFT background. Let \(\omega_{0,m}\) be the Poincare-invariant
    vacuum state of the free Klein-Gordon field \((\Box+m^2)\phi=0\). In the
    vacuum GNS representation, write \(\mathcal A(\mathcal O)\) for the local
    von Neumann algebra generated by the bounded local observables of the field
    localized in the bounded open region \(\mathcal O\). Equivalently, one may
    take \(\mathcal A(\mathcal O)\) to be generated by Weyl operators
    \(W(f)=\exp(i\phi(f))\) with test functions \(f\) supported in
    \(\mathcal O\), in the usual free scalar field net. Locality means
    \(\mathcal A(\mathcal O_A)\) and \(\mathcal A(\mathcal O_B)\) commute when
    \(\mathcal O_A\) and \(\mathcal O_B\) are spacelike separated.

    A bounded localization region is a nonempty bounded open subset of
    Minkowski spacetime, for example a double cone. Its closure is required to
    be compact. For two regions with spacelike separated closures, define their
    spacelike distance by
    \[
      \operatorname{dist}_s(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B)
      =
      \inf_{x\in\overline{\mathcal O_A},\,y\in\overline{\mathcal O_B}}
      \sqrt{|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^2-(t_x-t_y)^2},
    \]
    after restricting to pairs for which the expression under the square root is
    positive for all \(x,y\) in the two closures. The phrase "arbitrarily far
    apart" means that the asserted CHSH violation is required for every finite
    lower bound \(L\gt 0\) on this distance; the regions and observables may
    depend on \(L\), and no uniform positive lower bound on
    \(\Delta_m(L)\) is required.

    For two commuting local algebras
    \(\mathcal A_A=\mathcal A(\mathcal O_A)\) and
    \(\mathcal A_B=\mathcal A(\mathcal O_B)\), define
    \[
      \beta_{\omega_{0,m}}(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B)
      =
      \sup
      \left|
        \omega_{0,m}\!\left(
          A_1(B_1+B_2)+A_2(B_1-B_2)
        \right)
      \right|,
    \]
    where the supremum ranges over self-adjoint contractions
    \(A_1,A_2\in\mathcal A_A\) and \(B_1,B_2\in\mathcal A_B\), meaning
    \(A_i=A_i^*\), \(B_j=B_j^*\), \(\|A_i\|\le 1\), and \(\|B_j\|\le 1\).
    A CHSH violation means
    \(\beta_{\omega_{0,m}}(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B)\gt 2\).
    A proof using dichotomic local observables, \(A_i^2=B_j^2=\mathbf 1\), is
    sufficient, since these are special self-adjoint contractions.
  </def>

  <known>
    Open Quantum Problems, Bell Inequalities for long range vacuum
    correlations, Problem 12, asks whether some necessarily small violation of
    CHSH-Bell inequalities remains possible for regions arbitrarily far apart,
    and fixes the massive scalar free relativistic Bose field for definiteness.
    The Open Quantum Problems list records this as Problem 12 in the Quantum
    foundations category.

    Summers and Werner, "The vacuum violates Bell's inequalities", Physics
    Letters A 110(5), 257-259 (1985), prove maximal Bell-inequality violation
    for suitable spacelike separated observables in the vacuum of free quantum
    fields. The OQP Problem 12 page records this as the suitable spacelike
    observable phenomenon and also records that the violation goes to zero as
    the localization regions are moved apart. This entry does not claim that
    maximal violation persists at long distance; it asks only whether the
    optimized CHSH value remains strictly larger than \(2\) at every finite
    spacelike-distance threshold.

    Halvorson and Clifton, "Generic Bell correlation between arbitrary local
    algebras in quantum field theory", Journal of Mathematical Physics 41(4),
    1711-1717 (2000), prove that, under the Schlieder property, for commuting
    von Neumann algebras of infinite type, Bell-correlated normal states are
    norm dense in the normal state space of the generated bipartite algebra.
    They apply this framework to local algebras in algebraic quantum field
    theory. They also prove nonseparability results for vector states cyclic
    for one local algebra, and state that the Minkowski vacuum remains
    nonseparable across spacelike separated local algebras at all distances.
    Their results do not decide whether the vacuum itself has
    \(\beta_{\omega_{0,m}}\gt 2\) for arbitrarily distant bounded regions.

    Verch and Werner, "Distillability and positivity of partial transposes in
    general quantum field systems", Reviews in Mathematical Physics 17(5),
    545-576 (2005), formulate distillability and PPT-type conditions for
    general bipartite systems, including quantum fields, and show distillability
    phenomena for states such as vacuum and thermal states over spacelike
    separated subsystems under their hypotheses. These distillability and
    non-PPT facts are evidence of long-range quantum entanglement, but they are
    not themselves CHSH violations by local field observables. They are progress
    toward this entry only if combined with an argument proving
    \(\beta_{\omega_{0,m}}(\mathcal O_A,\mathcal O_B)\gt 2\) at arbitrary
    finite spacelike distance.
  </known>

  <ask>
    For the fixed mass \(m\gt 0\), prove or disprove the assertion that
    \(\Delta_m(L)\gt 0\) for every \(L\gt 0\). A proof must exhibit, or prove
    the existence of, bounded
    spacelike separated localization regions and admissible local observables
    for each finite \(L\) whose CHSH value in \(\omega_{0,m}\) exceeds \(2\);
    the excess may depend on \(L\) and may tend to zero as \(L\to\infty\). A
    disproof must prove that there exists a finite \(L_0\) such that no
    admissible pair of bounded local algebras separated by
    \(\operatorname{dist}_s\ge L_0\) has CHSH value greater than \(2\) in the
    vacuum state. Results proving only nonseparability, failure of a PPT-type
    condition, distillability after operations or postselection, generic Bell
    correlation for an open dense set of states, detector-mediated Bell
    violation, or maximal violation at bounded separation are progress, not a
    solution, unless they imply the stated inequality for
    \(\Delta_m(L)\) or its negation.
  </ask>

  <out>
    Return one of: proof, disproof, gap, progress.
    State all assumptions and cite every external theorem used.
  </out>
