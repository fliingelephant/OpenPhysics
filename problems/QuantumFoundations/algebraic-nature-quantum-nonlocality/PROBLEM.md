  <claim>
    For every triple of positive integers \(N,M,K\), the Euclidean closure
    \(\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\) of the finite-dimensional
    tensor-product quantum behavior set in the \(N\)-party, \(M\)-setting,
    \(K\)-outcome Bell scenario is a semialgebraic subset of the corresponding
    finite-dimensional real behavior space.
  </claim>

  <def>
    For a positive integer \(r\), write \([r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}\). In the uniform
    finite Bell scenario \((N,M,K)\), a behavior is an array
    \[
      p=\{p(a|x):a\in [K]^N,\ x\in [M]^N\}
      \in \mathbb R^{[K]^N\times [M]^N},
    \]
    where \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\) is the tuple of local settings and
    \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)\) is the tuple of local outcomes. Normalized
    conditional probabilities satisfy
    \[
      p(a|x)\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{a\in [K]^N}p(a|x)=1
      \quad\text{for every }x\in [M]^N .
    \]

    The finite-dimensional tensor-product quantum set
    \(\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\) consists of the behaviors \(p\) for
    which there exist positive integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_N\), a density operator
    \[
      \rho\in
      \mathcal D(\mathbb C^{d_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb C^{d_N}),
    \]
    and, for each party \(j\in[N]\) and setting \(x_j\in[M]\), a POVM
    \(\{E^{j,x_j}_{a_j}:a_j\in[K]\}\) on \(\mathbb C^{d_j}\) such that
    \[
      E^{j,x_j}_{a_j}\succeq 0,\qquad
      \sum_{a_j\in[K]}E^{j,x_j}_{a_j}=I_{d_j},
    \]
    and
    \[
      p(a|x)=
      \operatorname{tr}\!\left[
        \rho\,
        \bigotimes_{j=1}^N E^{j,x_j}_{a_j}
      \right]
      \quad
      \text{for all }a\in[K]^N,\ x\in[M]^N .
    \]
    The local dimensions are finite but are not bounded as a function of
    \(N,M,K\) in the definition of \(\mathcal Q^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\).
    Replacing finite-dimensional POVMs by finite-dimensional projective
    measurements does not change the set, by applying Naimark dilation locally
    to the finitely many POVMs.

    The closure \(\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\) is taken in
    the Euclidean topology of
    \(\mathbb R^{[K]^N\times[M]^N}\). In the bipartite notation common in the
    literature, this is the quantum-approximate set \(C_{qa}\), the closure of
    the finite-dimensional tensor-product set \(C_q\), for the corresponding
    two-party input-output sizes.

    A subset \(S\subseteq\mathbb R^D\) is semialgebraic if it is a finite
    Boolean combination of sets described by polynomial equalities and weak or
    strict polynomial inequalities with real coefficients, equivalently a
    finite union of basic semialgebraic sets. Since
    \(\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\) is closed, semialgebraicity
    would also allow a finite union of closed basic polynomial descriptions. A
    disproof must rule out all such finite semialgebraic descriptions, not only
    a single-conjunction presentation.
  </def>

  <known>
    Open Quantum Problems, Problem 34, "Algebraic nature of quantum
    nonlocality", asks whether for every \(N,M,K\) the closure of the
    multipartite set \(Q'\) of finite-dimensional tensor-product quantum
    correlations generated by local POVMs is semialgebraic, phrasing this as a
    finite polynomial-inequality characterization of membership in the closed
    set. The Open Quantum Problems index lists this as Problem 34, "The
    geometry of quantum nonlocality", in the Quantum foundations category, with
    W. Slofstra and M. Navascues as contacts and date 2017/04/26.

    Open Quantum Problems, Problem 34, records that the answer is positive for
    \(M=K=2\). Cirel'son proved the bipartite dichotomic reduction, and Masanes
    proved the all-\(N\) dichotomic-setting, dichotomic-outcome extremal-point
    reduction: extreme points are realizable by projective measurements on
    \(N\)-qubit pure states. Together with Caratheodory's theorem and the
    Tarski-Seidenberg theorem, this gives a finite polynomial description for
    the \(M=K=2\) quantum set.

    Slofstra, "The set of quantum correlations is not closed", Forum of
    Mathematics, Pi 7, e1 (2019), constructs a linear-system nonlocal game
    playable perfectly by a limit of finite-dimensional strategies but not by
    any finite-dimensional tensor-product strategy, and proves that the
    finite-dimensional tensor-product quantum correlation set is not closed in
    some finite bipartite scenario. This shows that the closure in the claim is
    essential. It does not by itself disprove semialgebraicity of the closure.

    Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright, and Yuen, "MIP*=RE", arXiv:2001.04383,
    prove \(MIP^\ast=RE\). Their arXiv abstract states as a consequence that
    \(C_{qa}\), the closure of finite-dimensional tensor-product correlations,
    is strictly contained in \(C_{qc}\), the commuting-operator correlation
    set, giving a negative answer to Tsirelson's problem and refuting Connes'
    embedding conjecture. This separation and the associated undecidability of
    gapped nonlocal-game values do not, by themselves, decide whether a fixed
    \(C_{qa}\) is semialgebraic over arbitrary real coefficients.

    Fu, Miller, and Slofstra, "The Membership Problem for Constant-Sized
    Quantum Correlations is Undecidable", Communications in Mathematical
    Physics 406, 96 (2025), Theorem 1.1, prove that for some integer
    \(\alpha\), for every fixed bipartite size
    \(n_A,n_B,m_A,m_B\gt\alpha\), membership in \(C_{qa}(n_A,n_B,m_A,m_B)\)
    over real algebraic numbers from the roots-of-unity field is undecidable.
    The same theorem also applies to \(C_{qc}\). This is a fixed-scenario
    undecidability theorem for the closure of finite-dimensional
    tensor-product correlations.

    Fu, Miller, and Slofstra's theorem refutes the rational, algebraic, or
    otherwise algorithmically usable semialgebraic strengthening of the claim:
    a finite list of polynomial equalities and inequalities with coefficients
    in a decidable ordered field, or with coefficient data accompanied by an
    exact sign-decision procedure for algebraic inputs, would give a decision
    procedure for membership of algebraic input correlations by exact sign
    evaluation and real-closed-field decision methods. The theorem is not
    recorded here as a formal disproof of the present claim because Open
    Quantum Problems, Problem 34, does not restrict the coefficients of the
    semialgebraic description to be rational, algebraic, or effectively
    sign-decidable. Semialgebraic sets with arbitrary real parameters need not
    give an effective membership algorithm for algebraic input points. Thus the
    unrestricted real-coefficient semialgebraicity question remains the
    canonical open version in this entry.
  </known>

  <ask>
    Prove or disprove the claim. A proof must show that for every positive
    \(N,M,K\), the closed set
    \(\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\) admits a finite
    semialgebraic description in the real behavior coordinates, with all
    quantifiers over states, local dimensions, and local POVMs eliminated.
    The coefficient field or parameter set of the resulting polynomial
    description must be stated explicitly.

    A disproof must exhibit a specific finite Bell scenario \((N,M,K)\) and
    prove that \(\overline{\mathcal Q}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{N,M,K}\) is not
    semialgebraic over real coefficients. A result proving only that no
    rational, algebraic, uniform, or otherwise algorithmically usable
    polynomial description with exact coefficient-sign data exists is progress
    on the effective strengthening, not a disproof of the claim as written,
    unless it also rules out arbitrary real-coefficient semialgebraicity.
  </ask>

  <out>
    Return one of: proof, disproof, gap, progress.
    State all assumptions and cite every external theorem used.
  </out>
</problem>
